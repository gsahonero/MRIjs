Section 01

Static Field Strength (B₀) and Nuclear Precession Frequency Dynamics

Atomic nuclei containing an odd number of protons or neutrons exhibit an intrinsic quantum mechanical angular momentum known as nuclear spin (\(\vec{I}\)). Proportional to this angular momentum is the magnetic dipole moment \(\vec{\mu} = \gamma \vec{I}\), where \(\gamma\) is the gyromagnetic ratio unique to each nuclide.

When placed into a powerful static main magnetic field \(\vec{B}_0 = B_0 \hat{z}\), the magnetic moment experiences a mechanical torque \(\vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu} \times \vec{B}_0\). Because angular momentum is conserved, this torque causes the vector to undergo gyroscopic precession around \(\hat{z}\) at the angular frequency given by the Larmor Equation:

$$\omega_0 = \gamma B_0 \quad (\text{rad/s}) \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad f_0 = \frac{\gamma}{2\pi} B_0 \quad (\text{MHz})$$ Equation 1: The Larmor relationship establishing linear proportionality between field strength B₀ and precession frequency f₀.

In our current live model, with the main field set to B₀ = 1.50 T, hydrogen protons (¹H, γ/2π ≈ 42.576 MHz/T) precess at 63.864 MHz, completing one full revolution every 15.66 ns. Because electron clouds slightly shield nuclei from B₀, protons in different chemical environments precess at shifted frequencies. For example, water and lipid protons exhibit a 3.5 ppm chemical shift separation (Δf = 3.5 × 10⁻⁶ × f₀), resulting in an offset of 223.5 Hz.

Section 02

Spatial Position Encoding via Linear Magnetic Field Gradients

Because an RF receiver coil detects only the integrated voltage induced across the whole sample, a uniform magnetic field yields zero spatial discrimination. To resolve anatomy, we introduce linear magnetic field gradients:

$$\vec{B}(\vec{r}) = \left(B_0 + \vec{G} \cdot \vec{r}\right) \hat{z} = \left(B_0 + G_x x + G_y y + G_z z\right) \hat{z}$$ Equation 2: The gradient magnetic field points along $\hat{z}$, but its magnitude varies linearly across space.

Consequently, the local Larmor frequency varies with coordinate \(x\): \(\omega(x) = \omega_0 + \gamma G_x x\). Spins at position \(x\) accumulate phase according to \(\phi(x, t) = \int_0^t \gamma G_x(\tau) x d\tau\).

In our live model below, with gradient G_x = 20 mT/m applied for 5.0 ms, edge isochromats diverge in frequency by ±851.5 kHz, causing the collective net transverse signal to dephase down to 18.4%.

Section 03

Resonant Radiofrequency Excitation and B₁ Nutation Dynamics

To tip the longitudinal net magnetization \(M_z\) into the transverse plane, an alternating RF current is applied perpendicular to \(\vec{B}_0\) at the Larmor frequency, creating a dynamic magnetic field \(\vec{B}_1(t)\).

In the rotating reference frame (moving at \(\omega_{\text{rf}} = \omega_0\)), \(\vec{B}_0\) is eliminated. The net magnetization undergoes steady gyroscopic nutation around \(\vec{B}_1\) through a tip angle \(\alpha\):

$$\alpha = \int_0^{T_{rf}} \gamma B_1(t) \, dt \qquad \stackrel{\text{Rectangular Pulse}}{\Longrightarrow} \qquad \alpha = \gamma B_1 T_{rf}$$ Equation 3: Flip angle $\alpha$ as a function of RF field amplitude $B_1(t)$ and duration $T_{rf}$.

Currently, for a flip angle of α = 90° and pulse duration 2.0 ms, the required peak RF amplitude is 2.9 µT. When paired with a slice-selection gradient (G_z), shaped pulses (such as sinc pulses) produce rectangular frequency profiles, yielding an excitation slice thickness of 1.47 mm (Δz = Δf / (γ G_z)).

Physical Insight: Counter-Rotating Field & The Bloch-Siegert Shift: A linearly polarized RF pulse decomposes into two circularly polarized components: a co-rotating field at $+\omega_0$ and a counter-rotating field at $-\omega_0$. While the counter-rotating field is too far off-resonance ($2\omega_0$) to tip the spins directly, quantum perturbation induces a small second-order frequency shift: $$\Delta \omega_{BS} = \frac{(\gamma B_1)^2}{2\omega_0}$$ Known as the Bloch-Siegert shift, this phenomenon is actively exploited in modern clinical MRI for fast, phase-based transmit sensitivity ($B_1^+$) mapping!

Section 04

Signal Reception, 2D k-Space Formalism, and Image Reconstruction

During signal acquisition, the received voltage S(t) represents the spatial integration of transverse magnetization over the sample. By applying orthogonal gradients, we trace a trajectory through k-space (spatial frequency space):

$$S(t) = \int \int \rho(x, y) \, e^{-i 2\pi [k_x(t) x + k_y(t) y]} \, dx \, dy$$ Equation 4: The fundamental MRI signal equation demonstrating that raw data is the continuous 2D Fourier Transform of spin density \(\rho(x, y)\).

The coordinates in spatial frequency space (\(\vec{k}(t)\)) are dictated by gradient integrals: \(k_x(t) = \frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\int_0^t G_x(\tau)d\tau\) and \(k_y(t) = \frac{\gamma}{2\pi}\int_0^t G_y(\tau)d\tau\). To form the anatomical picture, the computer computes the 2D Inverse Discrete Fourier Transform (2D IDFT):

$$\rho(x, y) = \int \int S(k_x, k_y) \, e^{+i 2\pi [k_x x + k_y y]} \, dk_x \, dk_y$$ Equation 5: 2D Inverse Fourier reconstruction mapping spatial frequency samples into real-space tissue contrast.

In the interactive studio below, notice that under Full k-Space, the central 6 × 6 region contains 96.2% of total signal power. Low-pass filtering preserves bulk contrast while blurring edges; high-pass filtering produces edge detection with zero contrast; undersampling induces wrap-around aliasing ghosts.

Module 01

Nuclear Precession & The Larmor Equation

Nuclei with non-zero spin have a magnetic moment $\vec{\mu} = \gamma \vec{I}$. In a static field $\vec{B}_0 = B_0 \hat{z}$, torque $\vec{\tau} = \vec{\mu} \times \vec{B}_0$ induces precession:

$$\omega_0 = \gamma B_0 \quad (\text{rad/s}) \qquad f_0 = \frac{\gamma}{2\pi} B_0 \quad (\text{MHz})$$

For hydrogen protons ($^1\text{H}$), $\frac{\gamma}{2\pi} \approx 42.576\text{ MHz/T}$. Frequency scales linearly with field strength.

Module 02

Magnetic Gradients & Spatial Encoding

To localize signals, small linear gradient fields are superimposed along $\hat{z}$:

$$\vec{B}(x) = (B_0 + G_x x)\hat{z} \implies \omega(x) = \omega_0 + \gamma G_x x$$

Each coordinate position $x$ precesses at a distinct frequency. Phase accumulates as $\phi(x, t) = \gamma G_x x t$.

Module 03

RF Pulses & The $B_1$ Magnetic Field

An oscillating field $\vec{B}_1(t) = 2 B_1^e(t) \cos(\omega_{rf} t)\hat{x}$ creates a resonant torque in the transverse plane. In the rotating frame at $\omega_{rf} = \omega_0$:

$$\alpha = \int_0^{T_{rf}} \gamma B_1(t) \, dt \quad \stackrel{\text{Rect}}{\Longrightarrow} \quad \alpha = \gamma B_1 T_{rf}$$

By engineering RF amplitude $B_1$ and duration $T_{rf}$, any flip angle $\alpha$ ($90^\circ$ excitation, $180^\circ$ refocusing) is generated.

Image Formation & 2D k-Space

The received signal $S(t)$ is the continuous 2D Fourier Transform of the object spin density $\rho(x, y)$:

$$S(k_x, k_y) = \iint \rho(x, y) e^{-i 2\pi [k_x x + k_y y]} dx dy$$
